What the Build Gets Right

These took real discipline across a ~190-page static build, and they're done properly.

  • Architecture that preserves ranking equity. Clean hub structure — /services/, /mobile-detailing/{city}, /mobile-ceramic-coating/{city}, /paint-protection-film/{city}, /cars/{make} — with descriptive slugs matching the old site, all within 2–3 clicks of home.
  • Semantic HTML throughout. One H1 per page, logical heading flow with no level-skips found, header/nav/main/footer/section regions, and a skip link on every page. FAQs use native <details>, so answers are in the HTML on load — crawlable and accessible.
  • Metadata done right. Unique titles and meta descriptions per page, canonicals pointing at the production domain, Open Graph tags including og:image, and a sitemap driven by a SITE_URL environment variable so staging and production both resolve correctly.
  • Performance as a design constraint. AVIF/WebP images, below-fold lazy-loading with the hero exempted and prioritized, self-hosted fonts, essentially zero third-party scripts, prefers-reduced-motion respected, and YouTube embedded as a thumbnail facade instead of a live iframe.
  • Conversion path built for how people buy detailing. "Book Now" (external booking app) as the visually dominant primary, "Instant Quote" as secondary, a compact sticky mobile CTA bar, and tel:/sms: links throughout.
  • Real, attributed proof at decision points. "1,000+ five-star reviews" and "since 1994" sit beside CTAs, and service pages carry named, located reviews ("Aleksey G., San Diego, CA") — exactly what §10 asks for.
  • Transparent pricing. Service pages show real package prices (Bronze $599 → Gold $799 on ceramic coating) rather than hiding cost behind a call — §10's strongest trust signal for a high-commitment purchase.
  • Genuine message match on local pages. The La Jolla page isn't a find-and-replace template — it carries neighborhood-specific copy (the Cove, coastal conditions) satisfying §11, and every page has one clear purpose with no competing offers, popups, or promo bars (§1, §8).
  • Correct staging hygiene. The mockup is noindexed with canonicals to production, so it can't compete with the client's live site while under review.

Findings — In Priority Order

Numbered by recommended fix order. Items 1–4 are the ones that materially affect the launch.

1

No structured data on any page

High impact§15

Zero JSON-LD across the site. For a local service business with this page inventory, the misses are significant: LocalBusiness (service area, hours, phone) site-wide, Service on service pages, FAQPage where the accordions already exist, and BreadcrumbList on sub-pages. The old Webflow site the client is migrating from is unlikely to have this either — it's an opportunity, not just parity.

Fix One Astro component per schema type, injected via the shared layouts — covers all ~190 pages in a single template change. Roughly a half-day of work.
2

No breadcrumbs despite a three-level, 190-page architecture

High impact§2 · §12

City pages sit three levels deep (e.g. /mobile-ceramic-coating/carlsbad) with no breadcrumb trail. Breadcrumbs strengthen internal hierarchy signals — which matters for carrying the old site's rankings over — and pair naturally with the BreadcrumbList schema from finding 1.

Fix A breadcrumb component in the Service/Location layouts, rendered as real links with schema markup. Same template pass as finding 1.
3

Every image is hotlinked from the client's old Webflow CDN

Launch blocker

All photography loads from cdn.prod.website-files.com — the CDN of the client's current Webflow site. The moment they cancel their Webflow plan after migrating, every image on the new site breaks. This is fine for a mockup; it cannot ship this way.

Fix Download all referenced assets, self-host in /public (or our own CDN), and rewrite the URLs — scriptable in one pass. Must land before the client cancels Webflow.
4

No analytics or event tracking on the build

High impact§17

The mockup ships with no measurement layer at all — no GA4, no Tag Manager, nothing. The site's conversion actions are mostly off-site or tap-based (external booking app, tel:, sms:), which are exactly the interactions that vanish from reporting unless events are wired up deliberately. Without them there's no baseline at launch and no way to evaluate the funnel per §17 — the same gap that makes finding 7's form question undecidable.

Fix Install GA4 (via GTM) before launch with events for: Book Now clicks (outbound to the booking app), Instant Quote clicks, tel: and sms: taps, and email clicks. A half-day including testing in DebugView.
5

Review-platform attribution lives only inside images

Medium§3 · §13

The "1,000+ five-star reviews on [Google logo]" pattern puts the platform name only inside an image, and those logos carry alt="". Screen readers and search engines see "five-star reviews on" and nothing else — the guidelines flag exactly this: meaningful text must not exist only in images.

Fix Give the Google/Yelp logos alt="Google" / alt="Yelp". Minutes of work in two components.
6

Legacy copy carried over on the /cars/ pages

Medium§3 · §4

The Tesla page H1 reads "Preserve the Timeless Elegance of Your Tesla with Our Mobile Detailing Services!" — long, salesy, keyword buried at the end. The newer templates get it right ("Mobile Ceramic Coating Services San Diego"); this looks like copy inherited from the old site.

Fix A copy sweep of the eight /cars/ pages: benefit-led H1s with the make + service + location pattern up front.
7

No on-site lead form — "Instant Quote" lands on a form-less contact page

Discuss with client§9 · §17

The contact page offers chat, external booking, call, text, and email — but no form. That may be deliberate (the booking app is the funnel), but the guidelines favor a low-friction capture for visitors not ready to pick a time slot, and form submissions are far easier to attribute in GA4 than tel: taps.

Fix Propose a short 4-field quote form (name, phone, vehicle, service) on /contact as a test — evaluated on lead quality, not just volume, per §9.
8

Staging noindex is a manual launch step

Launch checklist

The noindex, nofollow tag is hardcoded in BaseLayout.astro with a "remove at launch" comment. Correct for now — but it's a manual step that isn't mentioned in DEPLOY.md. If it ships, the site is invisible to Google.

Fix Add it to the launch checklist below and to DEPLOY.md, or gate it on the SITE_URL env var so production builds drop it automatically.

Launch Checklist

In order. Items 1–4 before DNS cutover; 5–7 immediately after.

  1. Migrate all images off the Webflow CDN and rewrite URLs (finding 3).
  2. Add structured data + breadcrumbs at the layout level (findings 1–2).
  3. Install GA4 with conversion events — Book Now, Instant Quote, tel:, sms:, email clicks — so the launch has a baseline from day one (finding 4).
  4. Remove the noindex tag in BaseLayout.astro and confirm SITE_URL is set to production (finding 8).
  5. Verify Core Web Vitals with PageSpeed Insights once live on the production domain — lab numbers couldn't be pulled during this audit (API quota).
  6. Submit the sitemap in Search Console and confirm the old URLs resolve (structure was preserved, so redirects should be minimal).
  7. Fix alt text on platform logos and sweep the /cars/ copy (findings 5–6).

Method

Audited against the agency's "SEO and CRO Web Design Guidelines" on August 5, 2026. Raw HTML was fetched and inspected for five core templates on the live Vercel deploy: homepage, service page (/services/ceramic-coating), location page (/mobile-detailing/la-jolla), vehicle page (/cars/tesla-expert-san-diego), and contact — plus robots.txt and the sitemap index. Checks covered titles, meta descriptions, canonicals, heading hierarchy, semantic regions, structured data, image formats/alt/lazy-loading, forms, CTAs, fonts, and third-party requests. The CRO pass additionally reviewed hero copy and above-the-fold hierarchy (§4), pricing transparency (§10), local message match on city pages (§11), interruption patterns and page focus (§8), interactive states (§7, §14), and measurement readiness — analytics and event tracking (§17). Lab Core Web Vitals were not measured (PageSpeed Insights anonymous quota exhausted); the performance score above reflects implementation signals in the markup.
