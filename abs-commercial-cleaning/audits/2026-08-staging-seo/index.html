Findings — In Priority Order

Finding 1 is a decision, not a task — everything else depends on it.

1

63 of the live site's 66 pages don't exist on staging

Launch blocker

The live site's rankings live on its 21 service pages, 9 facility pages, 6 location pages, and 23 blog URLs — the pages the July report shows gaining impressions. On staging all of them 404. Before any further build-out, the team needs to decide what this staging site is for: if it replaces the live WordPress site, the full page inventory must be rebuilt or the cutover will erase the SEO progress the client is paying for; if it's a dead prototype, retire it.

Fix Confirm intent with the team. If proceeding: port all 63 missing pages at their exact live URLs (or ship a complete 301 map), then re-run this audit.
2

City pages moved to new URLs with no redirects

High impact§2

The five rebuilt city pages live at /Locations/austin etc., while the live, ranking URLs are /commercial-cleaning-locations-texas/austin/. A changed URL without a 301 is a new page starting from zero. The new paths are also capitalized — /About, /Services, /Locations — inviting case-sensitivity duplicates and breaking the lowercase convention of every existing backlink.

Fix Either keep the live URL structure (preferred — it's already ranking) or lowercase the new routes and ship 301s from every legacy URL.
3

No page-level metadata: one title and description shared site-wide

High impact§15

Home, About, and Services all render the identical title ("ABS Commercial Cleaning") and the same meta description; only the Austin page has its own title. There are no canonical tags, no Open Graph or Twitter markup, and no structured data — the live site has all of these, so as it stands the rebuild would ship with less SEO than the site it replaces.

Fix Wire Next.js generateMetadata per route: unique title/description, canonical, and OG tags — then port the live site's LocalBusiness, Service, FAQPage, and BreadcrumbList schema.
4

No sitemap.xml and no robots.txt

High impact§15

Both return 404 on staging. The live site serves a proper sitemap index (page, post, category, local sitemaps) that Search Console depends on — launching without one means slower discovery of every migrated URL at exactly the moment fast recrawling matters most.

Fix Add Next.js sitemap.ts and robots.ts. Trivial once routes exist — but it must be on the checklist.
5

Most images have missing or empty alt text

Medium§13 · §14

43 of 58 images on the homepage, 63 of 69 on the Services page, and 25 of 29 on the Austin page have no usable alt attribute. The live site's images are properly attributed, so this too is a regression.

Fix Alt-text pass across all components: descriptive alt for photos, alt="" only for genuinely decorative shapes.
6

Placeholder wiring: /Services renders a single carpet-cleaning page

Medium

The main nav's "Services" link lands on a page whose H1 is "Commercial Carpet Cleaning in San Antonio" — a single-service template parked at the hub URL. City pages also carry thin H1s (just "Austin"). Template scaffolding is fine at this stage, but it confirms the build is early-prototype, not review-ready.

Fix Build the real services hub listing all nine services, and give city pages descriptive H1s ("Commercial Cleaning Services in Austin, TX") — the live site's versions are the reference.

Path to Launch-Ready

Gated: don't start item 2 until item 1 is answered.

  1. Decide the staging site's role — replacement for the live WordPress site, or retired prototype (finding 1).
  2. Lock the URL map to the live site's structure — lowercase, matching paths, plus a 301 map for anything that must change (finding 2).
  3. Port the full page inventory — 21 service, 9 facility, 6 location, 23 blog, and 5 standalone pages (finding 1).
  4. Implement per-page metadata, canonicals, OG, and schema to at least live-site parity (finding 3).
  5. Add sitemap.xml and robots.txt, and fix the alt-text and placeholder-content issues (findings 4–6).
  6. Re-run this audit, then plan cutover with Search Console monitoring from day one.

Method

Audited August 5, 2026 against the SEO sections of the agency's "SEO and CRO Web Design Guidelines." Migration completeness: all 68 URLs from the live site's sitemap index (page, post, category, local — 66 pages plus two non-page assets) were requested on staging following redirects; staging serves 308 trailing-slash redirects, and final status codes were recorded. Staging inspection covered the four reachable templates (home, About, Services, Locations/austin): titles, descriptions, canonicals, headings, structured data, Open Graph, image alt coverage, robots.txt, and sitemap presence. The live site's own audit (run the same day) serves as the parity baseline.
